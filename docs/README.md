## Project management approach (summary)

OctoAcme follows a lifecycle-based project model built on customer-first outcomes, iterative delivery, and clear ownership. Work moves through Initiation, Planning, Execution, Release, and Close/Retrospective, with the Project Manager and Product Manager coordinating delivery with developers, QA/testing, and stakeholders. Core artifacts such as the one-pager, backlog, definition of done, release notes, and risk register keep decisions and expectations transparent across the team.

Execution practices emphasize turning plans into small, shippable increments with explicit acceptance criteria and regular team rhythm. Teams use project boards and PR workflows to move work from backlog through QA to done, while CI-based checks, linting, tests, and review approvals protect quality. QA is continuous rather than end-loaded, combining unit and integration testing, smoke testing for critical flows, security scanning, and manual acceptance validation when needed.

Risk management and communication are structured so issues are visible early and escalated quickly. Risks are captured in a register with owner, impact, likelihood, mitigation, and status, then reviewed in recurring syncs. Communication follows a predictable cadence—team standups, PM/PdM alignment, and stakeholder updates—supported by standard status templates and a single source of truth for project state.

Release and learning complete the loop through disciplined pre-release checks, deployment/rollback readiness, and post-delivery reflection. Before release, teams confirm acceptance criteria, CI and security outcomes, smoke-test readiness, and stakeholder communication plans; after release or milestones, retrospectives identify what worked, what to improve, and who owns follow-up actions. This continuous improvement cycle helps OctoAcme reduce risk over time while steadily improving delivery performance.
